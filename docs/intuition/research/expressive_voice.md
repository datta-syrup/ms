# Expressive, human-sounding realtime voice for MapleSyrup — speech-to-speech vs. expressive TTS, humming, Hebrew, and legal notes (research completed 2026-11-01)

Scope: which voice technologies, as of late 2026, can make a realtime game companion sound human — laughter, sighs, enthusiasm, fillers, humming a melody — in colloquial Hebrew and English within a 1 s response budget (later 0.5 s). All claims below were checked against the opened page cited next to them; vendor marketing figures are labelled as such. Legal points are informational, not legal advice.

Working context taken from the user's memory store (not from web sources): the product rule is "fastest, most natural-sounding voice model available (probably not Claude)" for conversation, with Claude only for complex questions after a spoken "let me think"; paid services are acceptable for the prototype; voices should be easy to change/search, and character voices are planned; no provider switch or new paid service without the founder's explicit approval; engineering rule of robust, generic, data-driven code with no hard-coded phrases.

Headline, cross-cutting facts that shape every section:
- Hume is shutting down EVI and Octave TTS (access ends 13 Nov 2026) — drop it from consideration.
- OpenAI is deprecating its standalone TTS endpoint models (tts-1, tts-1-hd, gpt-4o-mini-tts shut down 6 Jan 2027) and pushing TTS use to the Realtime API.
- On the independent Artificial Analysis Speech-to-Speech Index, most frontier S2S models (GPT-Live-1, gpt-realtime-2.1, Gemini 3.8 Live) show 1.2–1.35 s time-to-first-audio; only a few (Eleven Realtime v1 Max 0.57 s, Phonic 1 0.61 s, Grok Voice 3.0 0.81 s, gpt-realtime-2.1-mini at minimal reasoning 0.85 s) are under the 1 s target.
- No S2S or TTS vendor documents controllable humming; ElevenLabs' [sings] tag is experimental and OpenAI's own ChatGPT voice prompt says "Do not sing or hum". Humming must be engineered as an asset/side-channel, not prompted.
- Hebrew is the main discriminator: ElevenLabs v4-family and Realtime v1 (90+ languages incl. Hebrew), Gemini Live/TTS (Hebrew listed, but a Sept–Oct 2026 developer thread reports recognition and language-drift problems on 3.8 Live), OpenAI Realtime (Hebrew works; anecdotal reports it is far better than OpenAI's TTS). Nova Sonic, Kyutai, Sesame have no Hebrew.

## KQ1 — Realtime speech-to-speech models (gpt-realtime / GPT-Live, Gemini Live, Hume EVI, Sesame, Kyutai, Nova Sonic, newer entrants)

### Takeaway
The live S2S field in Nov 2026 is: OpenAI (gpt-realtime-2.1, gpt-realtime-2.1-mini, and the full-duplex GPT-Live-1 at $0.05/min + backend), Google (gemini-3.8-live / extended-thinking, ~$0.84/hr audio, Hebrew listed, proactive audio permanently on, affective dialog removed), ElevenLabs' brand-new Eleven Realtime v1 (OpenAI-Realtime-compatible WebSocket, 90+ languages incl. Hebrew by reference, 0.57–0.62 s TTFA claimed), plus Phonic-1 and Grok Voice as fast English-centric options; Hume EVI ends 13 Nov 2026, Sesame has an iOS app but no documented API, Kyutai is EN/FR only, Nova 2 Sonic has 7 languages without Hebrew. None documents laughter/sighs as a controllable feature — expressiveness in S2S is emergent and prompt-steered, so it must be tested per model.

### Inferences
- Against a 1 s end-of-speech-to-first-audio target, the three "brand" S2S models (GPT-Live-1, gpt-realtime-2.1 at high reasoning, Gemini 3.8 Live) measured 1.18–1.35 s TTFA on Artificial Analysis' harness, i.e. over budget before adding MapleSyrup's own audio pipeline. The realistic sub-1 s S2S options are: gpt-realtime-2.1-mini at minimal reasoning (0.85 s; but lowest quality index, 54.4), Eleven Realtime v1 Max (0.57 s, Hebrew by reference, OpenAI-compatible so it can be swapped in behind the same client code), Phonic (0.61 s, Hebrew unconfirmed), Grok Voice 3.0 (0.81 s; languages not researched). Reasoning effort is the main latency lever on OpenAI: run chit-chat at low/minimal and route complex questions to Claude after the spoken "let me think", exactly as the founder planned.
- Gemini 3.8 Live is by far the cheapest (~$0.84/hr vs $4.5–10.8/hr) and has the best arena Elo of the set, but for Hebrew it carries documented recognition/language-drift complaints and no language-code parameter; the permanently-on proactive audio (model may decide not to answer) and the removal of affective dialog cut against the "emotionally mirroring companion" goal. It remains worth a Hebrew bake-off because of price.
- Full-duplex (GPT-Live-1) matters for the "initiates small talk while you play" behaviour: it can listen while speaking and stops reliably on barge-in, but the Agora test shows it may also answer background voices spontaneously — in a gaming room with Discord chatter this needs a push-to-talk or wake gate.
- Because no S2S vendor exposes laughter/sigh/filler controls, "human presence" in a pure S2S design depends on prompt steering and model temperament and must be A/B-listened per model and per language; a cascaded design (KQ2) gives explicit, data-driven control of non-verbals via tags, which also fits the no-magic-strings rule (tags live in the content data, not in code).
- Eleven Realtime v1's OpenAI-Realtime-compatible WebSocket means a provider-agnostic client is cheap to build; design the voice layer as a pluggable adapter so the founder can approve provider switches without code rewrites.

### Gaps
- OpenAI does not publish a language list for gpt-realtime-2.1 / gpt-live-1, nor latency figures; openai.com announcement pages and the GPT-Live developer guide were not fetchable (client errors), so launch dates (GPT-Live in ChatGPT: July 8, 2026 per Agora; API: Sept 10, 2026 per TestingCatalog) come from secondary sources.
- No independent, recent (2026) measurement of Hebrew quality on gpt-realtime-2.1 / gpt-live-1 or Eleven Realtime v1 was found; only a Dec 2024 anecdote for OpenAI and a Sept–Oct 2026 complaint thread for Gemini 3.8 Live.
- Eleven Realtime v1 pricing and official latency were not on the pages opened; Grok Voice 3.0 and StepAudio 3 Realtime (appearing in the AA index) were not researched for languages/pricing.
- Whether any S2S model will laugh, sigh, or use fillers on instruction in Hebrew is undocumented everywhere; needs an internal listening test.
- Sesame's current product status for developers (API or not) is unconfirmed beyond the iOS preview; CSM open-source release status after Feb 2025 not verified here.

## KQ2 — Expressive TTS for a cascaded pipeline (ElevenLabs v4/v3/Flash, Cartesia Sonic, Hume Octave, OpenAI gpt-4o-mini-tts, Google Chirp 3 HD / Gemini TTS, Azure)

### Takeaway
For a cascade (STT → fast LLM → TTS) the two strongest Hebrew-capable, tag-controllable TTS options are ElevenLabs Eleven v4 Turbo (#1 on the Artificial Analysis TTS arena, ~100 ms median inference / ~150 ms time-to-first-speech, 90+ languages incl. Hebrew, inline [laughs] [sighs] [whispers] [excited] tags, bidirectional streaming; ~$40/1M chars) and Google gemini-3.8-flash-tts (#5 on the arena, 130+ languages incl. Hebrew, inline `<laugh>` `<sigh>` `<gasp>` tags plus a per-turn `style` field, streaming PCM, ≈$16.5/1M chars, price doubling in 2027). Cartesia Sonic 3.6 (#4, Hebrew, fast) is a third candidate but its emotion/laughter docs are login-walled. Hume Octave (sunset 13 Nov 2026) and OpenAI gpt-4o-mini-tts (shutdown 6 Jan 2027) should not be designed in.

## KQ3 — Humming and singing: which models can, and practical approaches

### Takeaway
No mainstream realtime S2S or TTS vendor documents reliable on-demand humming: ElevenLabs' [sings] is explicitly experimental, Gemini TTS lists only human vocalizations (laugh/sigh/gasp/yawn/sob), OpenAI TTS offers no such control and ChatGPT's own voice prompt forbids singing/humming; the only "speaks and sings" API found (Xiaomi MiMo-V2-TTS) is Chinese-dialect-focused. Practical routes are therefore asset-based: pre-render hummed clips per melody in the companion's voice (singing-voice synthesis such as DiffSinger/OpenUtau voicebanks or Vevo2-style humming-to-singing, or voice-convert a human hum with Seed-VC), or record a voice actor, and play them from a data-driven asset table with crossfade — any of which also removes humming from the latency budget.

### Cited Findings
- ElevenLabs lists [sings] only among "Unique and special (experimental)" tags: "Some experimental tags may be less consistent across different voices. Test thoroughly before production use."; no humming tag — [ElevenLabs prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v3). The audio-tags blog does not list singing/humming/music tags — [ElevenLabs audio tags list](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/elevenlabs-audio-tags-list).
- Gemini TTS: inline "non-speech human vocalizations" (`<laugh>`, `<sigh>`, `<gasp>`, `<yawn>`, `<sob>`, `<cough>`), avoid sound-effect tags; singing/humming not mentioned — [Gemini speech generation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation).
- OpenAI ChatGPT Advanced Voice Mode system prompt: "Do not sing or hum." — [Nymbo/Official_LLM_System_Prompts](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Nymbo/Official_LLM_System_Prompts/blob/73ba69b7a4c68bf5cdc7f0cb7846577df90e7448/ChatGPT%20Advanced%20Voice%20Mode.md). (Consumer prompt; API behaviour on a developer's humming request untested here.)
- Xiaomi MiMo-V2-TTS (update May 28, 2026): "a large-scale speech synthesis model independently developed by Xiaomi" with free-form style instructions; "When singing, it can also accurately render pitch and rhythm, delivering natural and expressive performance"; "mimo-v2-tts is now officially available via API" with "free access for a limited time"; dialect support "Northeastern Mandarin, Sichuan dialect, Henan dialect, Cantonese, Taiwanese accent…"; no Hebrew, no latency, no humming control described — [Xiaomi MiMo docs](https://mimo.mi.com/docs/en-US/news/previous-news/v2-tts-release).
- Vevo2 (arXiv 2508.16332v3, 05 Mar 2026; CUHK-Shenzhen / ByteDance Seed / Amphion): "a unified framework for controllable speech and singing voice generation"; melody via a notation-free prosody tokenizer — "we render MIDI notations into instrumental sounds and then extract their prosody tokens"; task table includes "Humming to Singing" with inputs "text, humming wav, singer wav"; "We will release code and model checkpoints of this study at Amphion"; no latency/real-time figures; languages not stated — [arXiv Vevo2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16332v3).
- Seed-VC (open source, GPL-3.0): "zero-shot voice conversion", "zero-shot real-time voice conversion", "zero-shot singing voice conversion"; clones from "a reference speech of 1~30 seconds"; real-time "algorithm delay of ~300ms and device side delay of ~100ms" (benchmark table: 430 ms latency on an RTX 3060 Laptop GPU with the 25M-param tiny model); singing model whisper-base 200M params at 44.1 kHz, recommended 30–50 diffusion steps with f0 conditioning; "It is strongly recommended to use a GPU"; repository "archived by the owner on Nov 21, 2025. It is now read-only." — [Seed-VC GitHub](https://github.com/Plachtaa/seed-vc).
- OpenUtau for DiffSinger: "an open source singing voice synthesis GUI editor" providing "a DiffSinger renderer for OpenUtau"; "Anyone can record their singing voice and make their own DiffSinger voicebank"; phonemizers for "English, Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Russian and more" (no Hebrew); has an Editing Macros API and plugin system; headless rendering not described — [OpenUtau (fishaudio fork)](https://github.com/fishaudio/OpenUtau).
- Eleven Music can "Create complete tracks with AI-generated lyrics and vocals in a variety of languages", instrumentals, sectional generation and game soundtracks; humming/a cappella, melody or MIDI input, and generation speed are not mentioned — [ElevenLabs Music help](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/core-capabilities/music/what-can-i-generate-with-eleven-music/llms.txt).
- Sesame's CSM paper discusses paralinguistics and contextual expressivity but not singing — [Sesame research](https://www.sesame.com/research/crossing_the_uncanny_valley_of_voice).

### Inferences
- "Humming on request" through the conversational model is the least reliable route (experimental tag, policy-trained refusals, no pitch control) and would also burn latency; treat it as a stretch experiment only.
- A production-safe design: a melody asset table (track id → hummed clip(s) in the active companion voice, per voice/character) rendered offline. Offline rendering options, in order of practicality: (1) record a voice actor humming (cheapest, highest quality, trivially legal for the voice; melody rights still apply — KQ4); (2) hum once and voice-convert into each companion voice with a singing-capable VC model (Seed-VC-class, GPU, archived project → maintenance risk); (3) MIDI/score → singing synthesis with a DiffSinger voicebank or Vevo2-style model, using neutral syllables ("hmm", "la") so Hebrew phonemizers are irrelevant.
- Humming has no lyrics, so the Hebrew/English split does not matter for the hum itself; only the spoken lead-in ("the piano there is insane…") goes through the TTS/S2S voice. Timbre continuity between the speaking voice and the hummed clip is the quality risk; voice conversion from the same voice reference mitigates it.
- Because clips are pre-rendered, the "starts humming Ellinia" moment costs no inference time; the conversation manager only needs an audio-mixer primitive (duck speech → play clip → resume), which is generic code with the melody list as data.

### Gaps
- No vendor or paper gives a measured quality or latency figure for humming specifically; Vevo2's humming-to-singing has no reported metrics and its code release was still a promise in the March 2026 version.
- Whether gpt-realtime-2.1 / gpt-live-1 / Gemini 3.8 Live / Eleven Realtime v1 will hum when the developer prompt asks (versus refuse) is undocumented; must be tested.
- ElevenLabs Music's input modalities (melody/MIDI/reference audio) and licence for in-product use were not found on the page opened.

## KQ4 — Legal: humming a copyrighted game melody (Nexon's MapleStory BGM) in a commercial product

### Takeaway
(Informational, not legal advice.) A hummed rendition reproduces the musical composition (melody), a copyright separate from the recording and owned by Nexon/its music arm; Nexon's own creator guides permit soundtrack use only in non-commercial UGC (ad revenue on free platforms excepted) and forbid taking music assets out of the game, so a paid companion humming the BGM falls outside the permitted scope and would need a licence. De minimis and fair use are circuit-dependent and fact-specific — and the recognisability that makes the humming charming is exactly what defeats de minimis — so the design-safe options are original melodies (the founder is a pianist/composer), generic non-melodic humming, or a negotiated licence/partnership with Nexon.

### Cited Findings
- US Copyright Office Circular 50 (rev. 08/2023): "A musical composition and a sound recording are two separate works"; a musical-work registration covers music and lyrics (authorship "in terms of the lyrics, the music, and/or the musical arrangement"), not the recorded performance; the owner has "the exclusive right to make copies, prepare derivative works, sell or distribute copies" and to "perform or display the work publicly"; arrangements/new versions are derivative works — [Circular 50 (PDF)](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ50.pdf).
- *Newton v. Diamond*, 388 F.3d 1189 (9th Cir. 2004; opinion filed Nov 4, 2003, amended Nov 9, 2004): Beastie Boys licensed the recording but not the composition of a three-note flute sequence (~6 s); court held "Beastie Boys' use of the 'Choir' composition was de minimis"; test: "a use is de minimis only if the average audience would not recognize the appropriation" (from *Fisher v. Dees*: "so meager and fragmentary that the average audience would not recognize the appropriation"); dissent (Graber) would have found a triable issue because the composition was distinctive — [Sheppard Mullin case copy (PDF)](https://www.sheppardmullin.com/assets/htmldocuments/newton%20v.%20diamond.pdf).
- De minimis split for sound-recording sampling: Sixth Circuit *Bridgeport Music v. Dimension Films* (2005) — even a small distinctive piece "can constitute infringement" (no de minimis for samples); Ninth Circuit *VMG Salsoul v. Ciccone* (2016) — whether the taking is "so trivial that it lacks practical importance or economic value", a sub-second horn hit "inconsequential"; Fifth Circuit *Batiste v. Lewis* (2020) noted criticism of Bridgeport — [TalksOnLaw](https://www.talksonlaw.com/blog/a-split-on-de-minimis-copyright-infringement-in-music).
- Nexon Game IP Guide for Content Creators: UGC = "derivative works generated by creators based on NEXON Game IPs"; Section 4 "Commercial use of UGC is prohibited" (no compensation for creating UGC, no selling or paid access); Section 5 exception: "You may collect advertising revenues or donations by uploading your UGC to content platforms" that "anyone can access for free", and 5.1 says this "includes UGC that makes direct use of game soundtracks"; soundtrack use must credit "NECORD Music's YouTube Channel"; guide "may be revised without any prior notice" — [Nexon IP Guide](https://m.nexon.com/terms/1037).
- Nexon "Statement on Permissible Uses of VOD and Streaming" (effective 14 December 2015): "You may make limited, non-commercial uses of our content"; "You may not engage in commercial use of our content, which is a violation of our Terms of Service"; "It is not okay to put those videos or streams behind a paywall"; "It is not okay to take assets from our games (e.g. characters, art, voice, music, items)" and distribute them separately; third-party music needs the owner's permission — [Nexon Player Support](https://playersupport.nexon.com/hc/en-us/articles/206109746).
- Licensing primer for games: a sync licence "covers the composition itself (the melody, lyrics, and arrangement)"; a master-use licence covers the specific recording (not needed when re-performing); a mechanical licence covers "the reproduction and distribution of copyrighted music in physical or digital formats" (relevant if audio ships inside a download); composition rights "usually owned by the songwriter or their publisher"; indicative costs "$500 to $2,000 per track" (indie, Synchtank 2024) to "$5,000 to $50,000+ per track" (AAA) — [Foxi Music guide](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/music-licensing-for-game-developers-guide/). (Vendor blog; indicative only.)

### Inferences
- Humming "Ellinia" in the companion's voice is a new performance/arrangement of Nexon's composition: no master-use issue (no recording copied), but reproduction, derivative-work and (if bundled) distribution rights in the composition are implicated, and MapleSyrup is a paid product — Nexon's "non-commercial UGC" allowance does not cover it.
- De minimis is unlikely to help by design: *Newton* turns on whether the average audience recognises the appropriation, and the feature's whole purpose is that players recognise the tune. Fair use (transformative commentary) is an argument, not a safe harbour, and would be litigated in the market's jurisdiction (US courts split on related doctrines).
- Safer design alternatives: (a) original leitmotifs per town/character composed in-house (the founder is a pianist), which can themselves become MapleSyrup IP; (b) non-melodic or improvised "contented humming" triggered by mood, not by track; (c) seek a licence/partnership — Nexon credits NECORD Music as its official OST channel, and MapleSyrup's planned B2B relationship with studios is a natural place to negotiate soundtrack rights; (d) if the companion comments on music, it can praise the track and let the game's own audio play, rather than reproducing it.
- Generic humming also fits the no-magic-strings rule: the melody library becomes a licensed/original asset list, with rights status as a data attribute gating playback.

### Gaps
- Israeli copyright law (Copyright Act 2007 fair-use and incidental-use provisions) was not researched; relevant since the company is Israeli.
- No source addresses AI-voice humming specifically, nor whether Nexon has ever licensed BGM to third-party software; Nexon's current (2026) terms of service text was not opened — the VOD statement dates from 2015 and the IP guide is undated.
- The split between US circuits on de minimis concerns sampling of recordings; no post-2004 appellate case on hummed/short melodic quotations was found.

## KQ5 — Hebrew specifics: unvocalized text, pronunciation, slang/prosody, providers (incl. Israeli), niqqud/phonetic hints

### Takeaway
Unvocalized Hebrew is phonetically underspecified (vowels, stress), and the evidence shows it breaks weaker engines (Resemble needs nekudot; ElevenLabs Multilingual v2 is unintelligible) while the LLM-class engines (ElevenLabs v3/v4, Gemini TTS, OpenAI realtime voices, Microsoft Avri/Hila) are rated "good" but still show accent drift, mis-recognition and language switching in 2026 reports (Gemini 3.8 Live). Israeli academic work (HUJI's HebTTS, Phonikud's IPA G2P + ILSpeech corpus, Interspeech 2026) exists for diacritic-free Hebrew and small local models, and Ivrit.ai is the Israeli speech-data community; no Israeli commercial expressive TTS provider surfaced in searches. Mitigations: constrain language via system instructions, add niqqud or IPA for proper nouns and ambiguous words where the engine supports it (Chirp 3 HD IPA for he-IL verified), and run a native-speaker listening test on slang.

### Cited Findings
- HebTTS (Hebrew University, Roth, Turetzky, Adi; paper year 2024): modern Hebrew "rarely uses" diacritics, so readers "must infer pronunciation from context", making TTS mapping hard; proposes a diacritic-free LM-based TTS where "the language model (LM) operates on discrete speech representations and is conditioned on a word-piece tokenizer", trained on "in-the-wild weakly supervised recordings"; claimed "superior to the evaluated baselines" (MMS, Roboshaul, diacritic-based systems); code at github.com/slp-rl/HebTTS — [HebTTS project page](https://pages.cs.huji.ac.il/adiyoss-lab/HebTTS).
- Phonikud (Kolani, Melichov, Calev, Alper; v1 14 June 2025, v4 2 July 2026, "Accepted to Interspeech 2026"): Hebrew G2P producing "fully-specified International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) transcriptions" by "augmenting a base diacritizer", addressing underspecified features "such as stress"; introduces the ILSpeech corpus of "paired Hebrew audio, text, and expert IPA annotations"; "small, local TTS models with phonetic input from Phonikud approach large proprietary systems"; code/data/models released at phonikud.github.io — [arXiv 2506.12311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12311).
- Independent Hebrew TTS comparison (snapshot 22 March 2025): MiniMax voice cloning "Best" ("cloned voices sounded natural in Hebrew"); Edge TTS (Microsoft Avri, Hila) "Good"; Gemini 2.5 Flash Preview TTS "Good"; ElevenLabs v3 "Good" but "multilingual v2 is unintelligible"; Chatterbox "Poor" ("cloning didn't carry through to Hebrew"); Resemble AI "Poor" — "Needs nekudot (diacritics) for intelligible output"; lists Ivrit.ai as "Israeli speech technology" — [Hebrew-TTS-Providers](https://github.com/danielrosehill/Hebrew-TTS-Providers).
- Gemini 3.8 Live Hebrew (Sept–Oct 2026): mis-recognition of basic words such as כן, repeated requests for repetition, random mid-call language switching; Google staff: Hebrew supported, "do not support setting an explicit language code parameter", restrict language via system instructions; sibling threads on language_code being overridden and "accent drifts mid sentence" — [Google AI developer forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-live-3-8-feels-like-a-regression-for-hebrew/185811).
- Gemini TTS lists Hebrew for both 3.8 TTS models (no locale code given) — [Gemini speech generation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation); a June 2025 forum post noted Hebrew was then missing from Gemini TTS while NotebookLM handled Hebrew well — [Google dev forum](https://discuss.google.dev/t/hebrew-text-to-speech-using-gemini/192380).
- Chirp 3 HD: `he-IL` supported; custom pronunciations via IPA / X-SAMPA are available for he-IL; pause control excluded for he-il — [Chirp 3 HD docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/chirp3-hd).
- ElevenLabs: Hebrew (he) in v4, v4 Turbo, v3, v3 Conversational; not in Multilingual v2 / Flash v2.5 — [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models). Cartesia Sonic 3.6: Hebrew (`he`) among 44 languages — [Cartesia TTS models](https://docs.cartesia.ai/build-with-cartesia/models/tts).
- OpenAI: TTS voices "currently optimized for English" with Whisper-level language coverage — [OpenAI TTS guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/text-to-speech); developer reports of a "heavy American accent" in Hebrew TTS and much better Hebrew in realtime voices — [OpenAI community (2024)](https://community.openai.com/t/real-time-voices-for-tts-api-specifically-hebrew-support/1064592), [OpenAI community (2023)](https://community.openai.com/t/tts-1-and-tts-1-hd-in-hebrew-has-an-american-accent/500307).
- Azure: `he-IL` confirmed for STT (fast transcription, multilingual refinement, custom speech) and MAI-Transcribe-2; DragonHD expressive voices do not list he-IL — [Azure language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=tts), [Azure HD voices](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-services/speech-service/high-definition-voices).

### Inferences
- For MapleSyrup's content layer, keep a Hebrew pronunciation lexicon as data: game proper nouns and ambiguous homographs mapped to niqqud-ed or IPA forms, applied by a provider-specific renderer (IPA for Chirp 3 HD; niqqud-in-text for engines that read it; plain for S2S). Phonikud (open, IPA with stress) is a ready component for generating such hints automatically.
- Colloquial register (slang, fillers like "נו", "יאללה", "וואלה") is where LLM-class voices differ most; no benchmark covers it, so an internal blind listening panel of native speakers on a fixed script (greetings, excitement, sarcasm, instructions) per candidate engine is the only way to pick — and the harness can be reused for every provider swap.
- For S2S in Hebrew, write the system instruction to pin the language and register and to forbid switching, since Gemini 3.8 Live has no language-code parameter and OpenAI publishes none; log language drift as a metric.
- Local, small Hebrew models (HebTTS, Phonikud-fed TTS) are not yet a latency-and-quality match for the cloud engines on the evidence found, but they are a hedge for cost and privacy later.

### Gaps
- No 2026 side-by-side Hebrew naturalness evaluation of ElevenLabs v4, Gemini 3.8 TTS, Cartesia Sonic 3.6, OpenAI realtime, Eleven Realtime v1 was found; the only comparison is a March 2025 snapshot.
- Whether ElevenLabs/Gemini/Cartesia honour niqqud in input text or offer Hebrew pronunciation dictionaries was not verified from their docs.
- Ivrit.ai's current offerings (datasets, STT models, any TTS) were not opened; no Israeli commercial expressive-TTS vendor was identified in searches (results dominated by global vendors' Hebrew landing pages).
- Hebrew slang and prosody handling in any S2S model is undocumented; MiniMax (rated best for Hebrew cloning in 2025) was not researched for 2026 status, latency or realtime suitability.
