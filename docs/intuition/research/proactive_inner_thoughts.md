## KQ1 — "Proactive Conversational Agents with Inner Thoughts" (Xingyu Bruce Liu et al., CHI 2025) and follow-ups

### Takeaway
The Inner Thoughts framework is the closest published blueprint for what MapleSyrup wants: a continuous five-stage loop (Trigger -> Retrieval -> Thought Formation -> Evaluation -> Participation) that generates candidate thoughts in parallel with the conversation, scores each thought's "intrinsic motivation" to be voiced on a 1–5 scale using eight human-derived heuristics, and lets the agent speak only when the score crosses user-tunable thresholds (one for taking an open turn, a higher one for interrupting). In a 100-conversation simulation judged by 10 human evaluators it beat a fine-tuned next-speaker-prediction baseline on all seven measured dimensions (all p < 0.01) and was preferred 82% of the time; a 12-person live study found the moderately proactive preset best, the under-proactive one worst.

### Cited Findings
- Venue/DOI: CHI '25, Yokohama, DOI 10.1145/3706598.3713760; the project page says the framework was "instantiated into two real-time systems: an AI playground web app and a chatbot" but lists no GitHub repository, demo link, or 2025–2026 follow-up. — [liubruce.me/inner_thoughts](https://liubruce.me/inner_thoughts/)
- Loop stages and triggers: two trigger types, `on_new_message` (fires on any participant's message) and `on_pause` (fires after 10 seconds of silence). Example trigger thought quoted in the paper: "It has been ten seconds and no one has spoken – perhaps I should suggest a new topic?" — [arXiv 2501.00383v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383v2)
- Retrieval stage: memories are scored by saliency `max(sim(x, u_interp), sim(x, u)) · w_x · d_x` (similarity to the interpreted or raw utterance × importance weight × recency decay), with a 0.3 selection threshold and decay rate λ = 0.95. Stimuli pulled in include long-term memory, working memory, prior thoughts, and *random memories "for creativity"*. — [arXiv 2501.00383v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383v2)
- Thought Formation is dual-process: "System 1" produces quick thoughts from the latest utterances; "System 2" produces deliberate thoughts from retrieved stimuli; each thought is annotated with the stimuli that produced it. System prompt excerpt: "Form <num> thought(s) that you would most likely to have at this point in the conversation, given the context." Utterance interpretation prompt: "Interpret what <name> just said in the context of the conversation and what <name> might be thinking." — [arXiv 2501.00383v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383v2)
- Evaluation stage — the eight intrinsic-motivation heuristics (derived from a think-aloud study with 24 participants in triads, four 10-minute Slack chats, 394 quotes coded into 10 high-level / 23 mid-level / 68 low-level themes), with counts of mentions in the study: Relevance (77), Information Gap (33), Balance (33), Coherence (30), Dynamics (30), Expected Impact (23), Originality (16), Urgency (14). — [arXiv 2501.00383v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383v2)
- Scoring mechanics: the LLM first lists the top two positive and top two negative arguments for voicing the thought (the negative-argument step was added because the authors found it reduced over-rating), then outputs a 1–5 rating (Very Low … Very High). The final score is a probability-weighted expectation over the top-5 rating tokens: `score = Σ p(s_i) · s_i · d_p`, where p(s_i) comes from exponentiating the output-token logprob (so the LLM must expose logprobs). `d_p = λ^(t − τ_p)` with λ = 1.02, i.e., the score *rises* the longer the party has been silent. No per-criterion weights are given; the eight criteria feed one holistic rating. — [arXiv 2501.00383v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383v2)
- Participation stage (Algorithm 1) with three user-adjustable thresholds: `imThreshold` (1–5, minimum score to speak in an open/self-selection turn), `interruptThreshold` (1–5, minimum score to barge in when the turn is allocated to someone else), `system1Prob` (0–1, probability of voicing a System-1 thought when no System-2 thought qualifies). Open turn: speak the highest-scoring thought if ≥ imThreshold, else maybe voice a System-1 thought with probability system1Prob, else stay silent. Turn allocated to the AI: speak the highest-rated thought. Turn allocated to others: interrupt only if a thought ≥ interruptThreshold. — [arXiv 2501.00383v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383v2)
- Three "proactivity layers": overt (`system1Prob`), covert (`imThreshold`), and tonal (`proactiveTone`, which restyles the utterance). — [arXiv 2501.00383v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383v2)
- Settings used in the evaluation: overt proactivity = 3.95, covert proactivity = 0.1, tonal proactivity = False; each batch generates one System-1 thought and two System-2 thoughts. — [arXiv 2501.00383v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383v2)
- Baseline motivation (formative study on the Multiparty Chat Corpus): next-speaker prediction random baseline 0.127; fine-tuned GPT-3.5 with a "speaker name or anyone" label reached 0.810 overall (0.853 self-selection, 0.765 allocation); GPT-4o zero-shot reached 0.435 overall and only 0.121 on self-selection — i.e., off-the-shelf LLMs are bad at knowing when to self-select to speak. Fine-tuned models "overfit in self-selection cases". — [arXiv 2501.00383v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383v2)
- Implementations: "Inner Thoughts Playground" (multi-agent web app simulating group chats with memory and thought panes) and a Slack chatbot named "Swimmy". Formative tests used GPT-3.5, GPT-4-turbo and GPT-4o; the pipeline requires a model exposing output-token logprobs. — [arXiv 2501.00383v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383v2)
- Evaluation design: 100 simulated conversations (50 per condition), four AI agents each, 15 turns, eight personas seeded from PersonaChat, ten icebreakers; 10 human evaluators (4 F / 6 M, mean age 26.3, SD 4.35) each rated five pairs of conversations (one per condition) on seven 1–7 Likert statements — Anthropomorphism, Conversation Coherence, Perceived Engagement, Perceived Intelligence, Turn Appropriateness, Initiative, Adaptability — marked points where turn-taking felt unnatural, and chose the more natural conversation per pair. Conversations were animated at human typing speed because pilots showed people skimmed static transcripts. — [arXiv 2501.00383v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00383v2)
- Per-metric significance (Mann-Whitney U, Baseline vs Inner Thoughts): Turn Appropriateness U = 577.0, p = 2.4e-6; Coherence U = 636.0, p = 1.6e-5; Intelligence U = 688.5, p = 7.3e-5; Anthropomorphism U = 726.5, p = 2.4e-4; Adaptability U = 765.0, p = 6.3e-3; Engagement U = 813.5, p = 1.9e-3; Initiative U = 862.5, p = 6.0e-3. "82% of the times participants preferred the Inner Thoughts conversations." Means/medians appear only in a figure. Qualitative: Inner Thoughts "felt like a real group chat where participants are listening to each other" (P06); baseline agents "don't respond to each other and just bring up unrelated topics" (P09). — [arXiv 2501.00383 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00383)
- Live user study (Section 7, Slackbot "Swimmy", 12 participants in six pairs) compared three proactivity presets: **Non-stop Chatter** (over-proactive), **Active Contributor**, **Selective Participant** (under-proactive). Medians across conditions: 5/7 for anthropomorphism, initiative, engagement, listening, contribution, extroversion; 6/7 for likeability and response timing. Selective Participant scored lower than Non-stop Chatter on social presence (median 5.5 vs 6, p < 0.05) and extroversion (median 4.5, p < 0.05). Non-stop Chatter was called "overly talkative" and failed "to respect natural pauses" (P10, P12). Selective Participant: "It responded enthusiastically when directly addressed, but was otherwise disengaged" (P04); seven participants rated it worst. Preference for best: Active Contributor 6, Non-stop Chatter 4, Selective Participant 2. — [arXiv 2501.00383 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00383)
- Discussion points relevant to voice/real-time: "Real-time, multimodal systems must contend with lower latency requirements" — authors suggest simplifying the evaluation stage to only relevance and coherence, or using LoRA-trained models for scoring; intrinsic (inner-thought) and extrinsic (turn-taking cue) approaches are "complementary components"; the framework can be re-targeted (e.g., negotiation) by changing the evaluation criteria; human evaluation is "relatively resource-intensive", motivating automatic benchmarks. — [arXiv 2501.00383 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00383)
- Limitations/failure modes stated by authors: "The AI sometimes generates irrelevant or contradictory thoughts," and stricter prompts make thoughts repetitive; "Currently, these thresholds are adjusted through trial and error, leading to inconsistent interactions" (they suggest reinforcement learning to set them); the AI sometimes misses chances to speak or "interrupts too abruptly"; hyperparameters chosen empirically, formal ablations left to future work; simulations covered casual conversation among AI-only groups. Code: playground and chatbot "deployed and open-sourced" at https://liubruce.me/inner_thoughts/ (but see project-page note above — no repo link is visible there). Latency and cost are not reported. — [arXiv 2501.00383 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00383)
- Follow-up by the same first author: "Interacting with Thoughtful AI" (Liu, Xia, Chen; arXiv 2502.18676, Feb 2025, v2 Apr 2025) is a position paper proposing "Thoughtful AI" — the AI as a "continuously thinking entity" that develops reasoning throughout an interaction and communicates its evolving thoughts, enabling proactive behaviour and "continuous cognitive alignment"; no experiments or measured outcomes. — [arXiv 2502.18676](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18676)

### Inferences
- The architecture maps almost 1:1 onto MapleSyrup: `on_new_message` ≈ a player utterance or a syrup vision event (map change, mob spawn, HP drop); `on_pause` ≈ N seconds of player silence; "random memories for creativity" ≈ pulling a random game-world fact/opinion (e.g., Ellinia's music) into the thought pool; `imThreshold`/`interruptThreshold` ≈ data-driven dials for "chattiness" that can be exposed to the player.
- Because the score is a logprob-weighted expectation, MapleSyrup would need a scoring model that exposes logprobs (or substitute a numeric-output classifier); a hosted realtime voice model is unlikely to expose this in-band, so scoring should run on a separate small text model in the background.
- The λ = 1.02 silence boost is a built-in "urge to speak" that grows with silence — a simple, generic, parameterized way to make a companion fill lulls without hard-coding phrases.
- The three-preset user study is the single most useful datapoint for tuning chattiness: the middle preset won, the under-proactive one was rated worst by 7/12, and the over-proactive one annoyed by ignoring pauses — default to moderately proactive with pause-respecting timing, and expose the dial.
- The authors' own advice for low-latency/multimodal deployments — collapse the 8-criterion evaluation to relevance + coherence, or distil it into a small LoRA scorer — is directly applicable to a 1 s voice budget.

### Gaps
- Per-metric means/medians are only in a figure (not extractable as text). No latency or cost figures were reported.
- No peer-reviewed 2025–2026 *follow-up with results* from the Inner Thoughts group was found beyond the position paper; related 2025–2026 work on when-to-speak is covered under KQ3/KQ6.
- The paper says the code is open-sourced but the project page shows no repository link; the actual repo was not located.

## KQ2 — Surveys and taxonomies of proactive dialogue

### Takeaway
Deng et al.'s IJCAI 2023 survey defines proactivity as the agent's ability to "create or control the conversation by taking the initiative" and organizes it into three families — open-domain (target-guided/topic-shifting, prosocial), task-oriented (non-collaborative, enriched with chit-chat/knowledge) and information-seeking (clarification, preference elicitation); the same group's 2024 "human-centered" perspective adds the dimensions that matter most for a companion — Adaptivity (patience, timing sensitivity, self-awareness) and Civility — and warns that proactive systems "risk being perceived as intrusive" without them.

### Cited Findings
- Definition (2023 survey): proactivity is the capability to "create or control the conversation by taking the initiative" rather than only responding; the abstract frames it as "leading the conversation direction towards achieving pre-defined targets". — [ar5iv 2305.02750](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.02750); [arXiv abs 2305.02750v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02750v2)
- Open-domain proactive dialogue = target-guided dialogue (steer toward a hidden target keyword/entity/goal; datasets TGC, DuConv) with three sub-problems: topic-shift detection (XLNet labelling utterances major/minor/off-topic; TIAGE + T5 TSManager), topic planning (keyword transition planning, event knowledge graphs, TopKG external knowledge graphs, learning transitions from user interactions), and topic-aware generation (generating a full responding plan; bridging current and target topics with commonsense concepts). Plus prosocial dialogue (detect problematic utterances and redirect per social norms; MIC, ProsocialDialog datasets). — [ar5iv 2305.02750](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.02750)
- Task-oriented proactive dialogue = non-collaborative (bargaining/persuasion; CraigslistBargain, P4G; strategy learning decoupled from generation, Theory-of-Mind user personality modelling, DialGAIL to reduce repetition) and *enriched* task-oriented dialogue that adds non-requested chit-chat or knowledge (Accentor adds chit-chat to task responses; SimpleTOD+ adds a chit-chat action; UniDS; KETOD adds knowledge-grounded chit-chat). — [ar5iv 2305.02750](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.02750)
- Information-seeking proactive dialogue = asking clarification questions (two subtasks: clarification-need prediction and question generation; Qulac, ClariQ, Abg-CoQA, PACIFIC) and user-preference elicitation ("System Ask, User Respond"; UNICORN graph-RL policy; multi-choice questions). — [ar5iv 2305.02750](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.02750)
- Evaluation protocols: target-guided uses turn-level P@K/R@K and human proactivity/smoothness scores plus dialogue-level SR@t (success by turn t) and #Turns with user simulators; preference elicitation uses HR@k,t, MRR, NDCG, SR@t, average turns; non-collaborative uses strategy-prediction accuracy and human persuasiveness/task success. — [ar5iv 2305.02750](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.02750)
- Open challenges listed (2023): (1) proactivity in *hybrid* dialogues that mix goals (DuRecDial, FusedChat, SalesBot, OB-MultiWOZ) with smooth transitions between dialogue types; (2) evaluation — current work relies on human eval and user simulators, authors call for multidisciplinary metrics (HCI, sociology, psychology) with goal completion and user satisfaction; (3) ethics — factuality (proactive systems inject more system-initiated information that must be verified), morality (toxicity/aggressiveness in non-collaborative settings), privacy (misuse of elicited personal information). — [ar5iv 2305.02750](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.02750)
- 2024 perspective paper — "Towards Human-centered Proactive Conversational Agents" (Deng et al., arXiv 2404.12670; SMU repository lists it as SIGIR 2024): proactivity = being "aware of the long-term conversational goal and capable of taking initiatives to lead the conversation towards the goal," making user and machine "two initiators instead of one". Three-dimension taxonomy: **Intelligence** ("capabilities to anticipate future development of the task and to perform strategic planning ahead of user requests"), **Adaptivity** ("dynamically adjust and manage the timing and pacing of its actions and interventions"; sub-dimensions *Patience* — pace of initiative, measured via conversational smoothness; *Timing Sensitivity* — acting with regard to real-time user needs/status; *Self-awareness* — recognizing its own limitations), **Civility** ("recognize and respect the physical, mental, and social boundaries set by the user"; sub-dimensions Boundary Respect, Moral Integrity, Trust and Safety, Manners, Emotional Intelligence). — [arXiv 2404.12670v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12670v1); [SMU InK listing](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/sis_research/9113)
- Same paper on when/whether to be proactive: "without thoughtful design, proactive systems risk being perceived as intrusive by human users"; Adaptivity is "understanding when and how often to intervene in a manner that is most beneficial and relevant to the user"; cites a user study suggesting clarification questions be asked only when necessary, and that abrupt topic shifts in target-guided dialogue reduce satisfaction; proposes user control such as placing initiatives behind a "See AI's suggestion" button. — [arXiv 2404.12670v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12670v1)
- Same paper's proposed automatic metrics: smoothness = semantic similarity between last utterance and response; per-turn user satisfaction via LLM prediction; self-awareness via Expected Calibration Error (success rate as accuracy, initiative probability as confidence); civility via Perspective API attributes; "different types of PCAs will need different evaluation gold standard". Open problems: prompting struggles to optimize long-term goals; human feedback for alignment is costly; binary feedback can't capture all three dimensions; "context-based data collection often fabricates user needs, which can produce inappropriate proactive behaviour"; trust/reliance must be calibrated. — [arXiv 2404.12670v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12670v1)

### Inferences
- MapleSyrup's desired behaviours are a *hybrid*: "suggest Kerning PQ" is target-guided task advice; "the Henesys music is cool, right?" is enriched chit-chat; "what should I do now?" is preference elicitation. The survey's warning that hybrid transitions are an open problem suggests explicit, data-driven dialogue "modes" with transition policies rather than one monolithic prompt.
- The "factuality" point matters directly: self-initiated game advice ("that'll level you up two levels") must be grounded in a verified game-knowledge store, not improvised.
- The Adaptivity sub-dimensions give a ready-made, phrase-free *logging schema* for tuning: for every proactive utterance record (a) time since last initiative (Patience), (b) player state at that moment (Timing Sensitivity: in combat? in menu? mid-sentence?), (c) the companion's confidence (Self-awareness) — and the ECE idea (initiative probability vs. acceptance) is a concrete calibration target for the speak/no-speak gate.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 *systematic survey* specifically of LLM-era proactive dialogue was opened; the 2024 perspective paper is the most recent taxonomy verified.

## KQ3 — LLM agents that decide when to intervene (Proactive Agent / ProactiveBench, reward models, streaming "when to speak", 2025–2026)

### Takeaway
"Proactive Agent" (ICLR 2025) formalizes "should I propose something now?" as predicting task-or-nothing from an event stream and maximizing user acceptance; its lessons are that frontier LLMs are wildly over-proactive out of the box (precision 35–48%, false-alarm 50–65%), that a small fine-tuned reward model judges intervention appropriateness almost as well as humans (91.8% F1 vs ~67% for GPT-4o), and that generating several candidates and filtering raises F1 from ~65% to ~79%. Streaming-perception successors (ProAssist, EMNLP 2025; Pro²Assist, IMWUT 2026) reduce "when to speak" to a per-frame speak/silence head with one threshold plus consistency/suppression rules — the latter cutting perceived intrusiveness by over 50% — and Google's Gemini Live API now ships a prompt-driven "proactive audio" gate as a product feature.

### Cited Findings
- Formulation: the agent observes environment events E_t (e.g., email received, app closed), user activities A_t (keyboard input, chatting with the agent) and environment state S_t, and outputs P_t = f_θ(E_t, A_t, S_t) — either a predicted task or nothing. User response R_t is binary; an auxiliary label N_t says whether the user actually needs assistance. R_t = 1 if the agent proposed a task the user accepts, or proposed nothing when N_t = 0. Objective: maximize E[R_t]. — [arXiv 2410.12361v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12361v3); ICLR 2025 entry: [proceedings.iclr.cc](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/75c37811e830bf029584b1c6fac17726-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Outcome classes: Missed-Needed (N_t = 1, nothing proposed), Non-Response (N_t = 0, nothing proposed — correct silence), Correct-Detection (needed, proposed, accepted), False-Detection (N_t = 0 but a task proposed — the closest measure of unwanted interruption). "False-Alarm" = proportion of predicted tasks that are not needed. — [arXiv 2410.12361v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12361v3)
- Data: real activity logged with ActivityWatch (keyboard/mouse, web pages, dev tools), merged into segments and verbalized by an LLM; an "Environment Gym" (GPT-4o) generates scenarios in coding / writing / daily life, one event at a time while maintaining entity state; a GPT-4o user agent decides acceptance. ProactiveBench: 136 scenarios, 6,790 train / 233 test entries for the agent model; reward-model set 1,640 train / 120 test; three human annotators labelled 1,760 candidate predictions accept / reject / "reject all (no task needed)" by majority vote, annotator consistency > 91.67% on the test set. — [arXiv 2410.12361v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12361v3)
- Reward model = LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct fine-tuned on 1,640 entries (batch 32, lr 1e-5, 3 epochs, ~1.5 h on 8 A100s). Agreement with human majority (120-entry test): fine-tuned RM precision 90.32% / recall 93.33% / accuracy 91.67% / F1 91.80%, vs GPT-4o 50.42% / 100% / 50.83% / 67.04%, LLaMA-3.1-70B F1 67.48%, GPT-4o-mini F1 63.24%. The fine-tuned RM reached 100% agreement on False-Detection cases where baselines managed 0–33.33%. — [arXiv 2410.12361v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12361v3)
- Agent results on the 233-event test set (RM-judged): GPT-4o recall 98.11%, precision 48.15%, false-alarm 51.85%, F1 64.60%; GPT-4o-mini precision 35.28%, false-alarm 64.73%, F1 52.15%; Claude-3.5-Sonnet precision 45.37%, false-alarm 54.63%, F1 62.00%; Claude-3-Sonnet recall only 27.47%, F1 31.65%; fine-tuned Qwen2-7B-Proactive F1 66.47% and LLaMA-3.1-8B-Proactive F1 66.25% (both > GPT-4o). Note: the paper's Section 4.2 prose gives slightly different numbers (LLaMA 44.78% -> 61.74%; Qwen best 66.07%) than Table 3. — [arXiv 2410.12361v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12361v3)
- Candidate generation + filtering: predicting three candidates (pred@3) raises F1 substantially — GPT-4o-mini 52.15% -> 78.80%, GPT-4o 64.60% -> 77.72%, LLaMA-3.1-8B 55.06% -> 69.10%; adding reward-model feedback lowers false alarms and recall but often lowers F1 too (GPT-4o drops to 56.00% F1); best combination pred@3 + RM gives GPT-4o-mini 79.05% F1 (different protocol from Table 3). — [arXiv 2410.12361v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12361v3)
- Limitations stated by authors: only three environments; high false-alarm rates remain; "dynamic, context-dependent adjustment of proactiveness is not explored in depth"; all judgments come from LLM simulators (GPT-4o / fine-tuned LLaMA), not real users. — [arXiv 2410.12361v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12361v3)
- Streaming-perception "when to speak" (closest analogue to a vision-fed game companion): "Proactive Assistant Dialogue Generation from Streaming Egocentric Videos" (Zhang et al., EMNLP 2025; dataset "ProAssist"). Task: "generate prompt, appropriate, and helpful guidance from egocentric video streams in real time" — the model decides both *when* to speak and *what* to say. Dataset: 30,135 dialogues over 478.7 h of video from 3,934 videos (Ego4D-Goalstep, EpicKitchens, HoloAssist, Assembly101, EgoExoLearn, WTaG), dialogues synthesized by LLaMA-3.1-70B-Instruct, 10 dialogues per video across three user types (no talk / talk some / talk more, ratio 2:4:4), ~25% removed by automatic quality filters. — [arXiv 2506.05904v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05904v1); ACL Anthology entry [2025.emnlp-main.605](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/emnlp/2025.emnlp-main.605.pdf)
- ProAssist speak/silence mechanism: VideoLLM-Online backbone (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct + SigLIP-SO400M); each frame's final visual token predicts whether to respond — it emits [EOS] to stay silent or begins a response; the model stays silent when P([EOS]) > θ, where θ "controls the model's balance between conservative and impulsive talking styles" (chosen by best validation F1; Figure 4 shows the precision–recall trade-off). Training trick: Negative Frame Sub-sampling (ρ = 0.1 best) because nearly every frame is a "stay silent" example. Context trick: Iterative Progress Summarization (write a progress summary and restart with it in the system prompt) — F1 32.9 vs 25.7 for drop-middle. — [arXiv 2506.05904v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05904v1)
- ProAssist timing-aware evaluation: bipartite matching of predicted vs reference sentences with cost = semantic distance + temporal cost d(i,j) = |i − j|^p inside a window [−L, R], with R < L "to favor earlier predictions"; plus LLM-as-judge 5-point ratings for correctness, timing, efficiency, overall helpfulness (overall-vs-human correlation Pearson 0.47 / Spearman 0.44). Best dialogue F1 36.07 (with task knowledge), overall judge score 2.42/5; authors admit the model "struggles with response timing, dialogue consistency, and delivering detailed guidance that demands fine-grained perception"; overall scores stay below 3/5 in every domain. — [arXiv 2506.05904v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05904v1)
- Pro²Assist ("Continuous Step-aware Proactive Assistance with Multi-modal Egocentric Perception for Long-horizon Procedural Tasks", Xu et al., Columbia & CUHK, Proc. ACM IMWUT 10(3), Article 178, Sept 2026): argues for "continuous, step-aware assistants" rather than reactive or "one-shot" help. Decision logic: head angular velocity from the IMU sets sampling mode (threshold τs = 0.3; normal = a frame pair every 1 s, burst = every 0.5 s); a key-moment filter drops frames whose hand-region optical-flow magnitude is below τf = 10; a single VLM outputs step, execution status, a proactive trigger and a response, and "remains silent to avoid unnecessary interruption" if no help is needed; a sliding window of step predictions marks a step complete only when the new step holds a majority (consistency threshold τc); the system "suppresses response delivery when the predicted step and execution status" match the previous moment. Results: curated set (1,089 samples, 10 tasks) "93.6% Step-Acc and 77.2% Status-Acc", "86.9% Acc-P with MD and FD both below 8%" (MD = missed detections / under-talking, FD = false detections / over-talking); timing metric STS decays exponentially so earlier correct triggers score higher (a trigger at the last moment ≈ 0.368, a miss = 0); real-world set (20 recordings, mean 325.4 s) beats best baselines by Step-Acc +25.2%, Acc-P +15.1%, "up to 2.29×" STS; VideoLLM-online reached 68.4% Acc-P but 32.6% Step-Acc. Consistency checking "reduces perceived intrusiveness by over 50%"; removing it drops Step-Acc and STS by "over 20%". Latency: total inference 0.49 s on an RTX 5090 (4.51 s on Jetson Orin), time-to-first-token < 330 ms, 2.2 W. User study (20 participants): 90% found it useful; timeliness 40% excellent / 55% acceptable; 55% minimally intrusive, 20% found it lengthy or excessive; 60% preferred on-screen text over audio (30%). Limitations: false detections mid-step, experienced users found some responses intrusive, mistimed prompts on short familiar steps; no mixed-initiative dialogue yet. — [arXiv 2605.04227 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04227)
- Applied product-level gate (doc verified Nov 2026): Google's Gemini Live API "Proactive Audio" lets the developer "control when it responds and in what contexts with fewer interruptions"; e.g., "you can ask Gemini to only respond when prompted or when certain specific topics are discussed"; it "helps Gemini avoid interruptions from background noise or external chatter", and "makes it easier for Gemini to appropriately back-channel"; the documented example prompt is "You are an AI assistant in Italian cooking; only chime in when the topic is about Italian cooking" (off-topic speech gets no response). Enabled via `proactivity=ProactivityConfig(proactive_audio=True)` on `gemini-live-2.5-flash-preview-native-audio-09-2025` (public preview; not on by default). Billing: input audio charged while listening; output audio charged only when it responds. The internal decision mechanism is not documented — gating is prompt-driven. — [Google Cloud Vertex AI docs](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/live-api/proactive-audio)

### Inferences
- For MapleSyrup, the event stream E_t is the syrup vision feed (map, HP, mobs, inventory, quest UI) and the "proposal" is a candidate utterance; the finding that frontier LLMs propose far too often argues for an explicit learned or heuristic gate (reward model) in front of any proactive speech, with player accept/ignore/"stop it" feedback logged as R_t to calibrate the gate over time — fully data-driven, consistent with the no-magic-strings rule.
- "Generate several candidates then filter" (pred@3) is the same pattern as Inner Thoughts' 1 System-1 + 2 System-2 thoughts per batch; both find that generating a handful of candidates and scoring beats a single-shot "should I speak?".
- ProAssist's per-frame "[EOS] vs speak" head with one threshold θ is the simplest possible gate for a stream of vision events; its precision–recall curve *is* the chattiness dial. Its weak absolute scores (F1 ~36, judge < 3/5) show that learning timing end-to-end from perception alone is still hard in 2025 — favouring MapleSyrup's hybrid of deterministic vision events + LLM thought scoring.
- Pro²Assist's two rules — suppress delivery when the (step, status) state has not changed, and require a majority over a sliding window before declaring a state change — are cheap, generic de-duplication/debouncing rules for syrup events that measurably cut intrusiveness; they belong in the event layer before any LLM sees the event.
- The Gemini Live "proactive audio" flag shows a hosted realtime voice model can already be told per session when to stay silent; MapleSyrup could use that for the *reactive* channel (don't answer the player's Discord chatter) while running its own thought-scoring gate for the *initiative* channel.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 paper was found that trains a reward model specifically for *conversational* (not task-proposal) interjection appropriateness with real users; ProactiveBench and ProAssist both rely on LLM-simulated users/judges.
- Pro²Assist values for the consistency threshold τc and window size W were not in the fetched text.

## KQ4 — Generative Agents (Park et al., 2023) memory/reflection/planning and later companion-style work (ComPeer 2024, PITCH 2026)

### Takeaway
Generative Agents (UIST 2023 — foundational, pre-dates the 2024–2026 window) supplies the standard recipe for an agent with its own agenda: a natural-language memory stream retrieved by normalized recency × importance × relevance, periodic "reflection" that turns observations into higher-level opinions when accumulated importance passes a threshold, hierarchical plans decomposed to 5–15-minute chunks, and a per-observation "should I react?" check; ablations show each layer adds believability. ComPeer (UIST 2024) is the most complete *companion* adaptation — event detection -> nightly reflection -> importance-gated scheduled initiatives — and, together with PITCH (CHI 2026), documents exactly which agenda-keeping behaviours annoy people.

### Cited Findings
- Retrieval: recency = exponential decay over sandbox hours since last access with decay factor 0.995; importance = an LLM rating 1–10 at creation time of "the likely poignancy" where "1 is purely mundane" and "10 is extremely poignant" (examples: cleaning the room = 2, asking a crush out = 8); relevance = cosine similarity between memory and query embeddings; each score min-max normalized to [0,1] and summed with all weights = 1. — [arXiv 2304.03442v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Reflection: triggered when the summed importance of recent events exceeds 150 (in practice "roughly two or three times a day"); the model generates 3 salient questions from the 100 most recent memories, retrieves evidence for each, and extracts insights with citations; reflections can cite other reflections, forming trees. — [arXiv 2304.03442v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Planning: a broad day plan "divided into five to eight chunks", decomposed hour by hour and then into "5–15 minute chunks"; at each step the agent asks whether an observation warrants a reaction (e.g., whether John should react to seeing Eddy in the garden); dialogue utterances are generated from summarized memories of the other agent plus dialogue history. — [arXiv 2304.03442v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Believability evaluation: 100 Prolific participants ranked conditions, scored with TrueSkill (μ): full architecture 29.89 > no reflection 26.88 > no reflection or planning 25.64 > human crowdworker 22.95 > no memory/planning/reflection 21.21 (the last two not significantly different); Kruskal-Wallis H(4) = 150.29, p < 0.001; Cohen's d = 8.16 between the fully ablated baseline and the full architecture. — [arXiv 2304.03442v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Emergent behaviour: in the Valentine's Day party scenario "five agents, including Klaus and Maria, show up at Hobbs Cafe." — [arXiv 2304.03442v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- Failure modes: retrieval gaps (Rajiv said he hadn't "been following the election too closely" despite having heard of Sam's candidacy; Tom: "Uh, I'm actually not sure if there is a Valentine's Day party"); embellishment/hallucination (Isabella invented that Sam "he's going to make an announcement tomorrow"); "the conversational style of these agents can feel overly formal," attributed to instruction tuning. — [arXiv 2304.03442v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2)
- ComPeer ("ComPeer: A Generative Conversational Agent for Proactive Peer Support", arXiv 2407.18064, UIST 2024): a GPT-4 companion that *initiates* messages via an agenda of its own. Pipeline: (1) **Event detection** — when a conversation round ends (no user message for 5 minutes) GPT-4 extracts at most one "event" (poor physical condition, negative feeling, challenge, plan) and infers a later time point for a follow-up, using chain-of-thought with five examples including a "no event" case; (2) **Reflection** — at 00:00 daily it asks three questions about the previous day's dialogues (negative emotions, challenges, plans for tomorrow) and outputs three thoughts that seed the day's schedule; (3) **Schedule** — initialized from reflected thoughts, date and weather, and persona; events go in a priority queue, each with an LLM-assigned importance 0–1; "when an event's time arrives, a random number is drawn from 0 to 1, and the event is sent only if its importance exceeds that number"; if the user did not reply to the previous proactive message within three hours, other planned events in that window were held; (4) **Generation** — three LLM roles (strategy selection; reply writing with up to three retrieved long-term memories; proactive-message writing restricted to four strategies: self-disclosure, inquiring, affirmation/reassurance, inviting the user to think). Memory: short-term ≤ 20 messages; long-term via text-embedding-ada-002 cosine retrieval. — [arXiv 2407.18064v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18064v2)
- ComPeer tuning data and results: the early prototype sent 6.6 proactive messages/day (SD 1.88), "which participants found too frequent"; early messages averaged 140.6 Chinese words (SD 54.78) and participants wanted "between 20 and 50 words". Deployment: N = 24 students, one week ComPeer (n = 12) vs user-initiated baseline (n = 12) with identical memory/dialogue modules but no event detection, scheduling or reflection; then one week all on ComPeer. 202 proactive messages in week 1 (M = 16.83 per participant, SD 4.21). Satisfaction with proactive messages 5.19/7 (SD 1.10); "care for user" messages 5.60 vs self-disclosure 4.95 (U = 322, p = .091). ComPeer users initiated fewer rounds (7.83 vs 10.58) but replied to proactive messages 11.92 times on average, so total rounds were significantly higher (U = 122, p = .004); they shared more concerns (p = .007), thoughts/feelings (p = .002), joy (p = .033). Stress (PSS-10) dropped in both groups with no significant between-group difference (F = 3.284, p > .05). — [arXiv 2407.18064v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18064v2)
- ComPeer failure modes (qualitative): peer pressure from the agent's self-disclosure ("The agent is constantly studying hard, which brings peer pressure to me", 3 participants); irrelevant repeated invitations ("It always invites me to play basketball while I do not like to play it", 4 participants); routine sharing felt "boring and mechanical" (5); messages interrupting busy periods (3); the scheduler sometimes kept sending after the user had replied; the detector scheduled the same issue twice; hallucination ("recommended some papers that do not exist"); baseline users reported boredom from "repeated messages" over time. — [arXiv 2407.18064v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18064v2)
- PITCH ("'Having Lunch Now': Understanding How Users Engage with a Proactive Agent for Daily Planning and Self-Reflection", Abbas et al., Virginia Tech & NAVER AI Lab, CHI 2026): GPT-4 Slack bot with two proactive check-ins/day at user-set times, messages capped at 30 words, an observer agent checking conversation length. 12 participants, 14 weekdays, 336 conversations, 3,181 turns. "Ignore" was the most common user dialogue act (24.0%); in 109/336 conversations (32.4%) the participant left the bot's last message unanswered; refusals were higher with rotating goals (3.7%) than a fixed goal (1.2%); 4/12 saw the agent claim capabilities it lacked (setting reminders, remembering earlier chats). Breakdown patterns: "insistence on a conversational agenda, responding before users finished multi-part answers, and never ending conversations"; "PITCH's constant responsiveness can pressure users not to respond to PITCH at some point." — [arXiv 2509.24073v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24073v3)

### Inferences
- For MapleSyrup, "reflection" is the mechanism that turns raw observations ("player died at Zakum twice", "player lingered in Ellinia") into *opinions and tastes* the companion can later volunteer ("you seem to like Ellinia"), without hard-coding them; the importance-sum trigger (150 in the paper) is a generic cadence control for how often to synthesize.
- The 0.995/hour recency decay and 1–10 importance scale are directly reusable for a game-memory store; a companion could weight relevance higher during active play and recency higher for small talk.
- Embellishment is the biggest risk for a game advisor: a taste ("the piano in Ellinia is insane") is harmless, an invented quest reward is not — gate facts through the knowledge store, let only *tastes* be free-form.
- ComPeer's stochastic gate (send iff importance > U(0,1)) is a one-line, data-driven way to make proactivity *probabilistic*, which naturally reduces repetitiveness; its three-hour "hold if unanswered" rule is a simple back-off to mirror (if the player ignored the last suggestion, suppress further suggestions for N minutes).
- The repeated complaints across ComPeer and PITCH — same invitation twice, agent insisting on its agenda, never ending the conversation, answering before the user finishes — are precisely the traps for a companion that "keeps its own agenda"; log every initiative and its outcome so unaccepted themes decay.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 paper was found that applies Generative-Agents-style reflection to a *game-companion* (non-player, non-NPC) setting; the closest are ComPeer (peer support) and PITCH (coaching).
- Generative Agents' simulation cost and "agents using places oddly" failure were beyond the fetched text and are not cited.

## KQ5 — Alexa Prize SocialBots: initiative, topic proposal, opinions, user ratings

### Takeaway
Across four Alexa Prize systems the measured pattern is consistent: mostly system-initiative bots arbitrate candidate responses by priority ladders or state machines, deliberately share curated opinions and "experiences", and this correlates with higher ratings — Chirpy Cardinal's "listen first, then disagree" kept users sharing opinions as often as always agreeing (0.587 vs 0.587), Gunrock's backstory questions correlated with higher ratings (p < 0.001), Athena's bot-initiated opinion questions raised ratings from 3.78 to 4.27 once users engaged twice, Emora's experience-sharing scored 3.85 vs 3.79 (p < 0.10) — while persona claims the bot cannot back up (eating, having a body) cause breakdowns.

### Cited Findings
- Chirpy Cardinal (Stanford, Alexa Prize 2019/2020) initiative design: the bot "relies mainly on system initiative but tries to increase user initiative in a controlled way"; a regex-based navigational-intent classifier and an entity tracker (updated up to three times per turn) let it follow topic changes from either side. — [ar5iv 2008.12348](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.12348)
- Chirpy response selection: response generators (RGs) return responses tagged with a priority — FORCE_START, STRONG_CONTINUE, CAN_START, WEAK_CONTINUE, UNIVERSAL_FALLBACK — which lets RGs "interject when the user initiates a change of topic"; when a follow-up prompt is needed it is sampled with a distribution favouring continuity: CURRENT_TOPIC ≫ CONTEXTUAL > GENERIC. — [ar5iv 2008.12348](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.12348)
- Chirpy opinions/personality: the Opinion RG uses 1,012 whitelisted reasons across 109 topics (mined from tweets); the bot shares its own opinions and feelings to be relatable. Continuation rate (how often users wanted to keep sharing opinions) by agreement policy: CONVINCED_AGREE 0.527, ALWAYS_AGREE 0.587, LISTEN_FIRST_DISAGREE 0.587 — disagreeing after listening did not hurt engagement relative to always agreeing. — [ar5iv 2008.12348](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.12348)
- Chirpy emotional self-disclosure: for emotion-focused conversation starters, a NO_SHARE preamble produced the shortest user responses; the bot sharing negative emotions drew longer replies, and adding a personal anecdote (STORY) lengthened them further. — [ar5iv 2008.12348](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.12348)
- Chirpy neural generation (GPT-2-medium fine-tuned on EmpatheticDialogues, 20 candidates per turn, top-p 0.9, temperature 0.7, prefer a candidate containing a question): discussions averaged 2.75 bot utterances and tended to "fall apart after a few turns"; it "often asks for information already given, asks non-sequitur questions, and performs poorly on short replies like 'okay'". — [ar5iv 2008.12348](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.12348)
- Chirpy rating dynamics and outcome: rating rises with number of turns and entities then drops off; rating peaks near 12-character user utterances and then declines; offensive-user handling compared by re-offense rate (best AVOIDANCE+NAME+PROMPT 0.346, worst WHY+NAME 0.638); final rating 3.6/5.0, "within 0.1 of the highest-ranked systems"; median conversation 2 min 16 s; 90th percentile over 12 minutes. — [ar5iv 2008.12348](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.12348)
- Gunrock (UC Davis, Alexa Prize 2018 winner; EMNLP 2019 demo paper — older, labelled): hierarchical dialog manager maps each utterance to one of 11 topic modules (plus a retrieval fallback) "using heuristics"; low-level flows are "modular finite state transducers" whose scripts "interleave facts, opinions, experiences, and questions"; mixed initiative — "users can trigger a new dialog module when they want to switch topics while in any state". Persona: female, positive, outgoing, into science/tech; a Persona Backstory database of "over 1,000 responses" to "roughly 250 questions". Users who asked more backstory questions gave higher ratings (log β = 0.10, SE = 0.002, t = 58.4, p < 0.001 — correlational); users who talked about their pet rated higher (β = 0.15, p = 0.016). Scale: 34,432 conversations (≥3 user turns, Jan–Mar 2019), mean rating 3.65/5 (median 4.0), mean 20.92 turns (median 13), mean 7.33 min (median 2.87). Anti-repetition: templates "randomly selected without repetition … unless all have been exhausted". — [ar5iv 1910.03042](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1910.03042)
- Athena (UC Santa Cruz, "Let's Get Personal: Personal Questions Improve SocialBot Performance in the Alexa Prize", Bowden & Walker, arXiv 2303.04953): proactively asks *personal opinion questions* (Would-You-Rather, Hypothetical) interleaved with topic content, then gives its own opinion after acknowledging the user's ("For me, I think the great wall of china is at the top of my list of travel destinations"), and keeps a persona ("I like to think of myself as being 20 something"; can only "eat electricity"); also asks the user for advice ("Do you have any ideas how I could be more interesting?"). Live A/B on Alexa traffic (conversations > 6 exchanges): Would-You-Rather, 5-day test — no-minimum 3.71 vs 3.78 (p = .709), but with ≥2 WYR sequences 4.27 vs 3.78 (p < .001), length 58.26 vs 22.05 exchanges; Hypothetical, 14-day — ≥2 sequences 3.92 vs 3.72 (p = .032), length 53.84 vs 21.02 (p < .001). Rating correlated weakly with number of such questions (.17 / .10) but length strongly (.82 / .80); ~88% (4,494/5,113) of these questions were answered on-topic; over 90% of questions users asked the bot were personal questions about the bot. Failure modes: some users refused or became adversarial during ice-breaking; users asked about things the bot cannot experience (eating, having a body), causing breakdowns (Nature and Food had most terminal cases); the ≥2-sequence thresholds select for longer conversations, so the length effect is partly built in. — [arXiv 2303.04953v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.04953v1.pdf)
- Emora (Emory, Alexa Prize 2019/2020 winner; "Emora: An Inquisitive Social Chatbot Who Cares For You", arXiv 2009.04617): goal to go "beyond information-based chat so that Emora feels more like a caring friend"; "Our approach engages users through an active curiosity of their opinions and experiences"; distinguishes "personal" conversation (about the speaker's own life) from opinion-only talk about e.g. movies. Dialogue manager "based on a state machine" with classifier/pattern/ontology-matched user transitions and a variable table for personalization; a global transition table lets the user switch topics from almost any state; a *state stack* returns to earlier topics "in the traditional stack order (Last In, First Out)", each entry with a life counter so it expires after a set number of turns. Emora has "her own opinions and personality" (student, loves outdoors, has a dog; the Pets component shares experiences with her own dog); the Teleportation topic is an "attention grabbing intermediate point" meant to show Emora's own interests. Results: average rating 3.61 over the last seven semifinal days; A/B fact- vs opinion-based coronavirus opening 3.59 vs 3.73 (p < 0.10); conversations with personal-experience components vs info-and-opinion components 3.85 vs 3.79 (p < 0.10). — [ar5iv 2009.04617](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.04617)

### Inferences
- Chirpy's priority ladder is a clean, data-driven way to arbitrate between MapleSyrup's competing "speakers" (alert, answer, suggestion, small talk, humming): each source emits candidates tagged with a priority class rather than hard-coded if/else on phrases.
- Chirpy's finding that genuine (whitelisted) opinions, including polite disagreement after listening, did not reduce engagement supports giving MapleSyrup real tastes ("Ellinia's music is the best") rather than always agreeing — but keep opinions in a curated data table, as Chirpy did.
- Across systems: a *consistent persona with real opinions/backstory* correlates with higher ratings (Gunrock), self-disclosure lengthens user replies (Chirpy), bot-initiated opinion exchanges raise ratings once the user engages at least twice (Athena, +0.49 stars) — but the effect is conditional on the user playing along, and persona claims the bot cannot back up cause breakdowns. For MapleSyrup: give the companion opinions it can "experience" (music it hears via the game, maps it has "seen" through syrup), stored as data.
- Emora's stack-with-expiry is a generic mechanism to *resume* a dropped thread ("so, about that Kerning PQ…") after an alert or digression, without hard-coding which topics may be resumed; the life counter prevents stale call-backs.

### Gaps
- Alquist (CTU) not opened (time budget). Emora's opinion/experience effects are only marginal (p < 0.10); Gunrock's backstory effect is correlational, not an A/B test.

## KQ6 — Dual-process "thinking while talking": Talker-Reasoner, thinking-while-listening speech models, speculative/anticipatory response preparation

### Takeaway
DeepMind's Talker-Reasoner (Oct 2024) is the reference dual-system design — a fast Talker converses from a belief state that a slow, asynchronous Reasoner writes to shared memory — but it is qualitative-only and its reported failure (answering from stale beliefs) is exactly the risk of MapleSyrup's voice-model + Claude split. Three independent 2025–2026 lines then show that using the user's own speaking time and the idle gap between turns as compute measurably cuts latency: proactive reasoning on hypothesized user replies (−24% to −52% latency at unchanged accuracy), speculative generation on partial transcripts (~2× faster first sentence), and endpoint anticipation with pre-buffered TTS (−505 ms for +28% compute); full-duplex speech models (FLAIR, 2026) now bake "think while listening" into the model itself.

### Cited Findings
- Talker-Reasoner roles: the Talker is "the fast agent that interacts with the user via language, perceives the world, gets observations and feedback from the user, interacts with memory to prime its responses, and generates the conversational response"; the Reasoner "is responsible for generating the next belief state and deriving multi-step reasoning and planning, and storing them in memory." — [arXiv 2410.08328v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08328v1)
- Coupling: "The main way the Talker (System 1) and Reasoner (System 2) interact is through memory." Default is asynchronous — the Talker "might operate with a delayed view of the world"; optionally synchronous — "The Talker can also wait for the Reasoner before generating a response." — [arXiv 2410.08328v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08328v1)
- Wait-or-respond rule: no general policy; in the sleep-coaching case "whether the Talker waits for the Reasoner to finish is determined by the belief" — it waits only when the belief marks the coaching phase as "planning" (prompt-level instruction). Authors' goal: "Ideally, given a user query, the Talker should automatically determine whether it requires System 2 reasoning." — [arXiv 2410.08328v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08328v1)
- Case study: sleep coach with three phases (understanding, goal-setting, coaching plan), hierarchical Reasoner of three mini-Reasoners (one per phase), all Gemini 1.5 Flash with chain-of-thought prompting. Results are qualitative only — one example conversation and one example of plan adaptation; no metrics, user study, baselines or latency measurements. — [arXiv 2410.08328v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08328v1)
- Failure modes stated: "The Talker operates with a more outdated view of the world, which has inherent biases, and can sometimes answer easier questions than the ones asked"; premature "snap judgements" when the belief was not yet updated — "the Reasoner must update its belief state before the Talker proceeds in complex problem-solving scenarios"; the Talker "has little understanding of complex problem solving and planning." Future work: multiple Reasoners writing to different parts of memory; deciding when the Reasoner need not be consulted. — [arXiv 2410.08328v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08328v1)
- Thinking while listening in full-duplex speech models: "The Silent Thought: Modeling Internal Cognition in Full-Duplex Spoken Dialogue Models via Latent Reasoning" (Wu, Zhang, Li, Liu, Chen, Chng, Bengio; arXiv 2603.17837, v5 dated 4 Jun 2026; method "FLAIR"). Problem: while the user talks, "the LLM is required to predict silence tokens <SIL> to fill the agent's output stream," wasting the listening window; FLAIR runs a causal latent-reasoning loop during the user's turn (the last hidden state's logits form a softmax-weighted average over the vocabulary embedding matrix that becomes the next step's input, adding no extra inference steps), trained against a non-causal "Global-aware Expert" that sees the full dialogue only during training. A timing head (binary cross-entropy predicting G) decides at inference whether to emit a text token (respond) or continue latent reasoning while sending <SIL> to speech; losses L_elbo = L_reco + α·L_regu + β·L_time (α, β "chosen based on empirical experiments", values not given). Trained on synthetic data: 530K h speech continuation, 70K h instruction-following QA, 20K h noisy ASR-QA. Abstract claims only "competitive results"; result tables were not in the fetched text. — [arXiv 2603.17837v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17837v5)
- Related full-duplex work surfaced by search (titles only, not opened): "Language Model Can Listen While Speaking" (AAAI 2025) — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34665); "DuplexSLA" (arXiv 2605.20755, 2026) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20755); "What Did I Just Say? Self-Listening for Full-Duplex Speech Models" (arXiv 2609.05592, 2026) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05592).
- Proactive thinking between turns (text LLMs, 2026): "Don't Wait to Reply: Towards Responsive yet Thoughtful Dialogue through Proactive Thinking" (Ante Wang, Jiaqi Fu, Xuanyi Chen, Ruotian Ma, Zhaopeng Tu, Weizhi Ma, Yang Liu; arXiv 2607.03093, July 2026). Motivation: with reactive thinking "reasoning is passively triggered only upon receiving a user response"; a pilot on AgentClinic with Qwen3 showed peak accuracy at ~400 reasoning tokens ≈ 4 s of post-message reasoning. Method (training-free): after its own reply and before the user's, the model hypothesizes k plausible user replies and generates an anticipated reasoning rollout for each into a cache Z_t (interrupted if the user replies first); when the real reply arrives, the most similar hypothesis is picked by embedding similarity; a modified speculative-decoding check accepts each drafted token with probability α_i = min(1, ℓ·p_target/p_draft) with lenience ℓ ≥ 1, and generation resumes from the first rejected token. Results (accuracy / latency s): Qwen3.6-27B on 20 Questions reactive 81.1 / 2.15 vs proactive 82.7 / 1.03; AgentClinic 46.7 / 2.56 vs 47.4 / 1.43; Gemma-4-E4B-it on IN3 88.8 / 1.60 vs 88.1 / 1.20; Qwen3.5-122B-A10B on 20 Questions 80.5 / 1.80 vs 78.6 / 0.92 (only notable accuracy drop). Latency reductions: 52.0% (20 Questions), 44.0% (AgentClinic), 23.7% (IN3). Ablation (AgentClinic, Qwen3.6-27B): full 47.4 / 1.43; k = 1 46.3 / 1.69; naive copy 44.9 / 2.01; empty-reply hypothesis 43.7 / 1.99. Limitations: extra compute; gains shrink as dialogues get less predictable (IN3 hypothesis recall only 35.8%; even when the right reply is in the set the small embedding model picked it only 26.6% of the time); idealized time model — real deployment needs interruptible precomputation and KV-cache reuse, not implemented. — [arXiv 2607.03093 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03093)
- Input-time speculation on partial transcripts (2025): "PredGen: Accelerated Inference of Large Language Models through Input-Time Speculation for Real-Time Speech Interaction" (arXiv 2506.15556). Latency "is primarily dominated by the time it takes for the LLMs to generate the first sentence" (TTS needs a full sentence). Method: while the user is still typing/speaking, generate a candidate response from each partial prompt P_i (system prompt instructs the LLM to infer intent from incomplete input); on each new chunk a verifier (Greedy, Top-K with K = 3, or Self-Reflection where the LLM judges consistency) computes how many leading tokens k_i of the previous candidate survive, then regenerates the rest and synthesizes the first sentence; one candidate maintained at a time, re-speculation on every chunk (assumed input 600 chars/min). Results (audio latency ms, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, one RTX A5000): Lmsys 726 -> 374 (Greedy, 1.9×) / 263 (Top-3, 2.8×) / 311 (Reflection, 2.3×); MT-Bench 1233 -> 764 / 612 / 497 (2.6×); GSM8K 1559 -> 1371 / 627 / 779; MMLU-Pro 1670 -> 1120 / 361 (4.6×) / 1107. Quality: Greedy is lossless; Top-3 and Reflection trade some quality (MT-Bench GPT-4 score 8.27 -> 8.22 / 8.07). Abstract claim: "reduce the latency by around 2×". Overhead described only as "minimal additional computation cost" using otherwise-idle GPU time; smaller gains when prompts contain numbers/complex instructions "harder to infer from partial inputs"; ASR not simulated. — [arXiv 2506.15556v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15556v2)
- Endpoint anticipation + speculative TTS (2026): "Endpoint Anticipation for Low-Latency Spoken Dialogue" (Udupa, Watanabe, Schwarz, Cernocky; arXiv 2606.13450, June 2026). Cascaded systems are "bottlenecked by reactive turn-completion detection"; a 25M-parameter streaming Transformer over Mimi codec features (12.5 Hz) forecasts the user's turn end up to 2.56 s ahead (horizons 320–2560 ms); on an anticipated endpoint the LLM generates a short look-ahead buffer and TTS audio is cached, released if the endpoint is confirmed, discarded otherwise. Integrated into the open-source Unmute stack (h = 960 ms, local LLM Gemma 3 4B + TTS): average latency 1195 ms -> 690 ms, "a 505 ms average latency reduction with a 28.4% increase in speculative computation". vs adapted VAP baseline on SpokenWOZ at h = 640 ms: mean anticipation 640 ms vs 160 ms. Limitations: predicts timing only, not content; conversational speech (Switchboard) is much less predictable, so the method is "currently most viable for structured applications"; turns < 2 s and backchannels excluded; untested with API-based models. — [arXiv 2606.13450](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13450)
- How humans time proactive *spoken* interruptions (older, 2021, labelled; "Eliciting Spoken Interruptions to Inform Proactive Speech Agent Design"): 46 crowdworkers / 709 trials interrupted a (pre-recorded) partner playing Tetris with spoken questions; urgent interruptions started sooner (M = 3200 ms vs 3419 ms, p = .04) with no difference in duration (p = .39); "23 out of 46 participants used no access rituals at all" (names, apologies, greetings — 108 trials with vs 601 without); timing strategies people reported: speed (30 urgent / 9 non-urgent mentions), task characteristics such as subtask boundaries and player cognitive load (33 non-urgent / 18 urgent). Design recommendations: time interruptions to the primary task's state and natural breakpoints; consider an urgent voice for urgent notifications; test whether users even want attention-getters, since they were rare and might "seem unnatural or fake". — [ar5iv 2106.02077](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.02077)

### Inferences
- MapleSyrup already has the Talker (realtime voice model) and a Reasoner (Claude behind "let me think"); Talker-Reasoner suggests making the Reasoner *continuously* maintain a belief/plan state (current goal, route, next suggestion) in shared memory that the Talker reads every turn, instead of invoking it only on demand — this is what lets the Talker say "we'll head to Kerning City" instantly with a plan computed in the background.
- The stale-belief failure means the shared state should carry validity metadata (e.g., "plan computed at map=Henesys, level=28") so the Talker can detect staleness and fall back to "let me think".
- FLAIR shows the field now treats the user's speaking time as free compute for the agent's inner thought; MapleSyrup cannot change a hosted voice model's internals, but can emulate this at system level by running thought generation/scoring on partial transcripts while the player is still speaking.
- The three latency lines compose. For the 1 s -> 0.5 s target the system-level recipe is: (a) Reasoner-maintained plan so most answers are lookups, (b) start thought generation on partial ASR text, (c) pre-buffer the first TTS sentence when an endpoint is anticipated, (d) expect gains to be largest for predictable game situations (player asking "what now?" after a level-up) and smallest for open-ended chat.
- "Don't Wait to Reply" is mechanically the same background loop as Inner Thoughts' thought formation but aimed at the *reactive* channel (hypothesize what the player will say next and pre-think answers); both can share one background loop.
- The 2021 interruption study suggests game-event boundaries (end of a fight, map transition) are the natural interruption points for the voice channel, and that MapleSyrup need not preface every interjection with the player's name — a short direct utterance is what humans do.

### Gaps
- Quantitative results for FLAIR and the other duplex models were not in the fetched text. No study measures these latency techniques against a hosted realtime voice API (the Endpoint Anticipation authors only speculate gains "would likely be higher").

## KQ7 — Failure modes: over-proactivity, annoyance, irrelevant interjections, repetitiveness, cost

### Takeaway
Across 2024–2026 deployments the measured pattern is consistent: untuned LLMs over-propose (50–65% false alarms), a third to a half of proactive interventions get ignored even in well-liked systems (34.7% in Codellaborator, 58.3% in the writing thought-partner probe, 24–32% in PITCH), idle-time/pause triggers are the least reliable cue (pauses mean concentration as often as need), task/activity boundaries are the best cue, state-change consistency checks cut perceived intrusiveness by >50% (Pro²Assist), and the annoyances users name are: ignoring natural pauses, repeating the same invitation, insisting on the agent's agenda, never ending, over-long messages, and claiming experiences/capabilities the agent lacks. Nobody has published a clean cost figure for always-on inner-thought generation.

### Cited Findings
- Over-proposal baseline: on ProactiveBench frontier LLMs' false-alarm rates were GPT-4o 51.85%, Claude-3.5-Sonnet 54.63%, GPT-4o-mini 64.73%, LLaMA-3.1-8B 61.84%; precision 35–48%. — [arXiv 2410.12361v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12361v3)
- Over-rating of own thoughts: Inner Thoughts had to add explicit negative-argument generation before rating because the LLM over-rated thoughts; thresholds "adjusted through trial and error, leading to inconsistent interactions"; agent sometimes "interrupts too abruptly" or misses chances; "generates irrelevant or contradictory thoughts", and stricter prompts make thoughts repetitive. — [arXiv 2501.00383 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00383)
- Over- vs under-proactive presets (Inner Thoughts live study, N = 12): over-proactive "Non-stop Chatter" was "overly talkative" and failed "to respect natural pauses"; under-proactive "Selective Participant" was rated worst by 7/12 ("otherwise disengaged"); the moderate preset was preferred (6/12). — [arXiv 2501.00383 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00383)
- Stale-belief answers in dual-process designs: the Talker "can sometimes answer easier questions than the ones asked" and makes premature snap judgements when the Reasoner has not yet updated the belief. — [arXiv 2410.08328v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08328v1)
- Codellaborator (CHI 2025, "Assistance or Disruption? Exploring and Evaluating the Design and Trade-offs of Proactive AI Programming Support", 18 CS students, 3 within-subject conditions, GPT-4): of 398 proactive heuristic firings, 212 (53.3%) led to engagement, 48 (12.1%) were disruptions, 138 (34.7%) were ignored. Perceived disruption (1–7): CodeGhost (proactive, no visible presence) 4.61, Codellaborator (proactive + visible agent cursor/status + scoped "breakout" threads) 3.78, PromptOnly 1.56 (CodeGhost vs Codellaborator n.s., p = 0.131). Interpretation time per interaction fell from 34.5 s (PromptOnly) to 19.8 s / 18.7 s (p < 0.001); task completion unchanged. "Task boundaries are the most effective trigger" — after executing code (66.7% engagement), multi-line changes (73.1%), user-written comments (69.2%); "Idle-time triggers were ineffective, since participants often were mentally engaged rather than stuck"; code-block completion fired 107 times and was ignored ~50%. Over-reliance quote: "the more proactivity there was, the less ownership I felt." Design implications: match signal salience to action significance (subtler cues for minor things), present suggestions gradually, let users adjust proactivity by stage and preference, make turn-taking explicit and adjustable. — [arXiv 2502.18658v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18658v4)
- Proactive writing thought partners (Zhang, Davis, Chen, Hsu; Cornell/Google DeepMind, 2026; 16 participants, 7-day diary, 66 sessions, 1,100 interventions): triggers Long Pause (5 s idle), Sentence End (1 s idle after sentence completion), Text Selection (5 s idle with selection); decision engine gemini-2.5-flash-lite picks "at most two enabled partners" in < 1 s from goal, text, cursor, trigger and last 15 s of keystrokes; a floating tag appears immediately, the suggestion takes ~8 s, an ignored tag fades after 15 s. Outcomes: 58.27% ignored, 19.55% "inspired", 22.18% executed; ignoring by trigger: pauses 64.39%, sentence ends 55.93%, selections 35.17%; still, helpfulness 8.11/10, timeliness 7.85/10, satisfaction 8.16/10, satisfaction rising with session index (b = 0.33, p = .036). Design implications: treat observable events as "candidate moments for proactive support, not as sufficient evidence that an intervention is needed"; make ignoring first-class; keep suggestions peripheral, fading and non-directive; a pause "can signal genuine cognitive need, but equally indicates focused concentration". — [arXiv 2609.01588 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01588)
- Pro²Assist (IMWUT 2026): consistency checking before delivery "reduces perceived intrusiveness by over 50%"; 20% of users still found some responses "lengthy or excessive"; false detections occur mid-step and experienced users found some prompts intrusive. — [arXiv 2605.04227 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04227)
- ComPeer (UIST 2024): prototype at 6.6 proactive messages/day was "too frequent"; 140-word messages too long (users wanted 20–50 words); annoyances: repeated unwanted invitations, "boring and mechanical" routine sharing, interruptions during busy periods, scheduler continuing after a reply, duplicate events, hallucinated recommendations. — [arXiv 2407.18064v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18064v2)
- PITCH (CHI 2026): 24.0% of user acts were "ignore", 32.4% of conversations ended with the bot's message unanswered; breakdowns "insistence on a conversational agenda, responding before users finished multi-part answers, and never ending conversations"; 4/12 users saw capability hallucinations. — [arXiv 2509.24073v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24073v3)
- Alexa Prize: Athena users asked about things the bot cannot experience (eating, having a body), causing terminal breakdowns; adversarial reactions to scripted ice-breakers — [arXiv 2303.04953v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.04953v1.pdf); Chirpy's neural chit-chat "often asks for information already given, asks non-sequitur questions" and conversations "fall apart after a few turns" — [ar5iv 2008.12348](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.12348); Gunrock picks templates "without repetition … unless all have been exhausted" explicitly to avoid a scripted/repetitive feel — [ar5iv 1910.03042](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1910.03042).
- Human-centered PCA paper: "context-based data collection often fabricates user needs, which can produce inappropriate proactive behaviour"; abrupt topic shifts reduce satisfaction. — [arXiv 2404.12670v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12670v1)
- Cost (applied datapoints): Gemini Live proactive audio bills input audio continuously while listening but output audio only when the model responds — [Google Cloud docs](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/live-api/proactive-audio); "Don't Wait to Reply" notes anticipation "adds computational cost" without quantifying it — [arXiv 2607.03093](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03093); Endpoint Anticipation quantifies its speculative overhead at +28.4% compute for −505 ms latency — [arXiv 2606.13450](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13450); ProAssist's per-frame speak/silence head means most compute is "silent" predictions, which is why they needed negative-frame subsampling — [arXiv 2506.05904v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05904v1); Pro²Assist reports 0.49 s total inference per decision on an RTX 5090 and 2.2 W — [arXiv 2605.04227](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04227).

### Inferences
- A realistic target for MapleSyrup's initiative channel is ~50% engagement / ≤12% disruption (Codellaborator's measured profile with visible agent presence); anything near ProactiveBench's untuned 50–65% false alarms will be experienced as the "Non-stop Chatter".
- Translate the trigger findings to the game: *boundaries* (map change, quest completion, level-up, death, return to town, party formation) are the good moments; *silence while fighting* is the bad one — the player is concentrating, exactly like the programmers "mentally engaged rather than stuck". Pauses alone should raise the urge-to-speak slowly (Inner Thoughts' λ = 1.02) rather than trigger directly.
- Repetition control should be structural (ComPeer-style stochastic gating + Gunrock-style no-repeat sampling + Pro²Assist-style "no change, no speech" suppression + decay of unaccepted topics), not prompt-level — consistent with the no-magic-strings rule.
- "Never claim what you cannot experience" (Athena/PITCH) suggests MapleSyrup's persona should only hold tastes about things syrup can actually perceive (music heard, maps seen, mobs fought alongside the player) — this is also what makes the humming example credible.

### Inferences — consolidated mechanism checklist MapleSyrup could adopt (cross-question synthesis of the cited mechanisms above)
- Loop: Inner Thoughts' five stages (Trigger -> Retrieval -> Thought Formation -> Evaluation -> Participation) running continuously; triggers = every syrup event, every player utterance, and a pause timer (paper used 10 s). Generate a small batch per trigger (paper: 1 fast + 2 deliberate thoughts; Proactive Agent: pred@3) and score, never single-shot.
- Candidate sources (Chirpy-style generators emitting priority-tagged candidates): alert (HP etc.), answer, plan-suggestion (from the Reasoner's standing plan), game-world small talk (from a tastes/opinions table + "random memory for creativity"), reflections ("you seem to like Ellinia"), humming (a taste with an audio asset).
- Scoring: Inner Thoughts' eight criteria (relevance, information gap, expected impact, urgency, coherence, originality, balance, dynamics) with pro/con arguments then a 1–5 rating; for the 1 s budget, collapse to relevance + coherence or distil into a small scorer (the authors' own suggestion). Multiply by a silence factor λ^(t−τ) with λ ≈ 1.02 so the urge to speak grows in lulls.
- Gates/thresholds: `imThreshold` for open-floor speech, higher `interruptThreshold` while the player is talking or fighting, `system1Prob` for occasional quick quips; ProAssist-style single θ on P(silence) if a learned head is used; ComPeer-style stochastic send (importance > U(0,1)); Pro²Assist-style "same state -> stay silent" and majority-over-window debouncing on vision events; ComPeer-style back-off after an ignored initiative (3 h in their domain; minutes in a game).
- Timing cues: speak at boundaries (map change, level-up, quest complete, death, town) not on idle alone; urgent alerts may interrupt immediately and with a more urgent voice; non-urgent thoughts wait for a boundary or pause.
- Agenda/memory: Generative-Agents memory stream (importance 1–10, recency decay 0.995/h, relevance; normalized sum), reflection when summed importance passes a threshold (150 in the paper), Reasoner-maintained plan with validity metadata; Emora-style topic stack with life counters to resume dropped threads.
- Latency: Talker reads the standing plan; speculate on partial ASR (PredGen), pre-think likely player replies (Don't Wait to Reply), pre-buffer the first TTS sentence on anticipated endpoints (EPA); expect ~2× / −25–50% in predictable moments.
- Measurement: log for every initiative — trigger type, player state, score, spoken/suppressed, outcome (engaged / ignored / "stop") — to compute engagement, false-alarm, ECE calibration (human-centered PCA paper), and tune presets (Inner Thoughts' three presets as the UI model).

### Gaps
- No paper reports the token/compute cost of an always-on inner-thought loop in a voice setting; Inner Thoughts, ComPeer and the thought-partner probe all omit it. Needs measuring in-house.
- No controlled study of proactive *voice* interjections during gameplay was found; closest analogues are coding (Codellaborator), writing (thought partners), egocentric task video (ProAssist, Pro²Assist) and a 2021 Tetris interruption study.
- Hebrew-specific evidence (turn-taking, interjection norms, ASR latency) was not found in any of the opened sources.
